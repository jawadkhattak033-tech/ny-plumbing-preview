Local conditions

Plumbing Problems We See Most in the New York City

Corroded risers in prewar buildings

Galvanized and cast iron risers rust from the inside, choking pressure on upper floors and leaking through ceilings. Replacing a riser section takes careful planning so the rest of the line stays in service.

Stack backups in tall buildings

When a shared waste stack clogs, the lowest units on the line get the overflow. Grease, wipes and old pipe scale are the usual causes, and a camera shows where the blockage sits.

Old house traps and street sewers

Many brownstones and rowhouses still have a house trap near the front wall. When it clogs or the line to the street sags, the cellar floor drain is the first place to back up.

Low pressure on upper floors

Worn booster pumps, failing pressure reducing valves and scale in branch lines leave top floor tenants with weak showers. We trace the cause before recommending any equipment.
