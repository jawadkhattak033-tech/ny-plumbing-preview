Every commercial kitchen sends fats, oils and food solids down the drain, and the grease trap is what keeps them out of the sewer. As the trap fills, grease hardens on top and solids settle on the bottom until there is little room left for water to pass. That is when odors start, floor sinks slow down and backups happen.

We service small under sink traps and larger in ground interceptors. Cleaning means removing all the grease and solids, scraping the walls and baffles, and checking that inlet and outlet pipes are clear. We also look for cracked lids, missing gaskets, damaged baffles and leaking fittings, because a broken trap lets grease pass straight into your drain line.

After each visit you get a written record of the date, the condition of the trap and what was removed. Many restaurant owners keep these records on hand for health inspections, landlords and local sewer authorities. Over several visits, those records also show how quickly your trap fills, which helps us set a cleaning interval that fits your menu and volume instead of guessing. If your kitchen adds a fryer or extends its hours, we can adjust the schedule to match.

Warning signs

Signs Your Grease Trap Needs Service

  • A rancid or sour smell near the dish area or floor sinks.
  • Three compartment sinks drain slowly during cleanup.
  • Grease or food bits appear in floor drains or floor sinks.
  • Water backs up around the trap lid or cleanout.
  • It has been longer than usual since the last pumping.
  • An inspector or landlord has asked for service records.

How it works

How We Service a Grease Trap

Schedule Around Service

We set a time before prep or after close, so the kitchen is quiet and the trap has had a chance to settle.

Remove Grease and Solids

We pump or hand clean the trap completely, scrape grease from the walls and baffles, and remove the settled solids from the bottom.

Inspect and Repair

We check baffles, lids, gaskets and connecting pipes. If anything is cracked or leaking, we explain it and price the repair before doing it.

Record the Visit

We reassemble the trap, run water to confirm flow and leave a dated service record you can keep for inspections.

Local know how

Grease Trap Requirements in New York Communities

Many cities and sewer districts in New York require food service businesses to keep grease out of the public sewer, and inspectors may ask how often your trap is cleaned. Rules and pumping schedules vary by municipality, so we recommend checking with your local sewer authority. We will gladly provide records that help you show regular maintenance.

Cold weather matters too. Grease hardens faster in pipes running through cold basements and along exterior walls in winter, which can clog the line between the trap and the sewer. Kitchens that are busy during the holidays often need more frequent service in the colder months.

Pricing

What Drives the Cost of Grease Trap Service

Cost depends on the size and type of trap, how full it is and how hard it is to reach. A small under sink unit is quicker to clean than a large in ground interceptor. Traps that have gone a long time between cleanings take more labor, and repairs to baffles or fittings are priced separately.

Regular scheduled service usually costs less over a year than emergency cleanouts. We give you a firm price before every visit.
