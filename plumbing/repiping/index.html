Quick answer: Call Brightwater Plumbing and Drain at (518) 555-0142 to schedule a repiping assessment. A technician checks your pipe material, pressure and water quality, then lays out a whole house or partial plan with a firm price. Most homes keep water on overnight while the work moves forward.

Pipes do not last forever. Galvanized steel rusts from the inside until the opening is a fraction of its original size. Some early copper develops pinhole leaks after decades of use. Polybutylene, a gray plastic used for a period in the past, is known for failing at its fittings. When leaks keep showing up in different places, repairing one spot at a time stops making sense.

Repiping replaces the supply lines that carry clean water to your fixtures. We plan the new routes before we start, often running new pipe through basements, closets and existing chases so we open as few finished walls as possible. Most homeowners choose PEX for its flexibility and fewer joints, or copper where it fits the home better.

We work in sections, connect each finished section before moving on, and clean up at the end of each day. You will know which rooms are affected and when. Before the job starts, we walk you through the route the new pipe will take and mark every spot where a wall or ceiling needs a small opening, so nothing comes as a surprise.

Warning signs

Signs Your Home May Need Repiping

  • Brown or rust colored water when you first open a tap.
  • Water pressure is weak on several fixtures, not just one.
  • You have had pinhole leaks in more than one spot.
  • Exposed pipes show heavy rust, flaking or green crust at joints.
  • Your home has gray polybutylene or original galvanized supply lines.
  • A renovation will open walls where old pipe already runs.

Local know how

Why So Many New York Homes Need Repiping

A large share of homes in the state were built well before plastic supply pipe was common. Rowhouses, farmhouses and postwar Capes often still carry original galvanized steel, sometimes joined to newer copper in a patchwork that grew over decades. Where those metals meet, corrosion speeds up.

Water chemistry plays a role as well. Some areas have hard well water that scales pipes from the inside, while others have softer water that can be harder on copper over time. Add years of freeze and thaw cycles in basements and crawlspaces, and many older homes reach a point where new lines are the steadier choice.

Pricing

What Drives the Cost of Repiping

The size of the home and the number of bathrooms and fixtures set the scope. Pipe material matters, since copper and PEX differ in labor and parts. Access is a big factor too. An open basement is simple, while plaster walls, tile and finished ceilings take more care. Permit requirements in your town also affect the total.

We price the whole plan before any work starts, so you know the scope and cost up front instead of discovering it room by room.
