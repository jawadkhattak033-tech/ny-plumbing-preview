A plumbing emergency is any problem where waiting makes it worse. Water spreading across a floor, a sewer line pushing waste back into the house, a water heater leaking from the tank, or a home with no running water at all. In those moments the first few minutes matter more than anything a plumber does later.

That is why our first job happens on the phone. We help you find the main shutoff, kill power to an electric water heater, or keep people away from a sewage backup while the truck is on the way. Many callers have the leak stopped before we arrive, which limits the cleanup and the repair.

Once on site, the goal is to make the home safe and working again. Sometimes that is a full repair on the spot. Other times it is a solid temporary fix that holds until parts arrive. Either way, we explain the options and the price before we touch anything. When we leave, you know what failed and what to watch.

Warning signs

When to Call an Emergency Plumber

  • Water is spraying, pouring or dripping through a ceiling or wall.
  • Sewage or dirty water is coming up through a floor drain, tub or toilet.
  • You have no water anywhere in the house during cold weather.
  • Your water heater is leaking from the tank or the relief valve will not stop.
  • You smell gas near a gas water heater or other gas appliance.
  • The main shutoff will not close or is broken.

Local know how

Why New York Emergencies Spike in Winter

Deep cold snaps are the busiest nights of the year for emergency plumbing across the state. Pipes running through outside walls, crawl spaces and unheated porches freeze first, and they often do not leak until the ice thaws the next morning. Seasonal homes and camps left without heat are especially at risk.

Spring brings a second wave. Snowmelt and heavy rain push groundwater up against basement walls, and a sump pump that sat idle all winter can fail just when it is needed most. Older city neighborhoods with combined sewers also see more backups when storms overload the system.

The biggest factors are what failed, where it is and what it takes to reach it. A split pipe in an open basement ceiling is a quicker job than one inside a finished wall. Parts matter too, as does whether the repair can be finished on the first visit or needs a temporary fix first.
