When a pipe bursts, every minute counts. A split supply line can push out a surprising amount of water, and it soaks into drywall, flooring and insulation quickly. The first step is always the same: find your main shutoff and close it. If you cannot find it or it will not turn, tell us when you call and we will talk you through it while a technician heads your way.

Once on site, we confirm the water is off, open the area enough to see the damage and cut back to sound pipe on both sides of the break. We replace the section with matching material or an approved transition fitting, then bring the pressure back slowly and watch the repair before closing anything up.

We also check what caused the break. Freezing, corrosion, high water pressure and old fittings all fail in different ways. A pipe that froze needs protection from the cold, while a pipe that rusted through may have neighbors close behind it. If the rest of the line looks weak, we tell you plainly so you can plan.

Warning signs

Signs of a Burst or Broken Pipe

  • Water dripping from a ceiling, light fixture or wall seam.
  • A sudden drop in water pressure throughout the house.
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off.
  • Bulging, stained or soft drywall that was not there yesterday.
  • Puddles in the basement with no clear source.
  • The water meter keeps moving with all taps closed.

How it works

How We Repair a Burst Pipe

Stop the Water

We confirm the main or the nearest branch valve is closed and drain the line so the repair area stays dry while we work.

Expose the Break

We open only as much ceiling or wall as needed to see the damaged pipe and the sound pipe on either side of it.

Cut Out and Replace

The split section is removed and replaced with matching pipe and fittings, joined to code so the repair is as strong as the original line.

Restore and Watch

Pressure comes back slowly while we check every joint. We explain the cause and whether any other sections need attention soon.

Local know how

Why Pipes Burst in New York Homes

Freezing is the leading cause here. A pipe that froze during a January cold snap often bursts days later, when the ice melts and pressure returns. That is why burst pipe calls tend to spike on the first mild afternoon after a hard freeze, not on the coldest night itself.

Older housing adds to the risk. Many homes in the state still have galvanized steel that has rusted thin, or early copper with joints that have seen decades of expansion and contraction. Homes on municipal systems with high street pressure can also stress older fittings until one gives way.

Pricing

What Drives the Cost of Burst Pipe Repair

Where the pipe broke matters most. An exposed basement line is the simplest repair. A break inside a finished ceiling, behind tile or under a floor takes more time to reach. The length of damaged pipe, the material, the number of fittings involved and an after hours call also affect the price.

You get the price before we start the repair. Wall and ceiling patching is a separate trade, and we will tell you what we opened so your contractor or insurer has a clear picture.
